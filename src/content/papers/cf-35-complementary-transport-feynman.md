## Abstract

Complex multiplication, coherent addition, and quadratic readout are related ingredients of quantum calculation, but they answer different reconstruction questions. We separate them on a supplied reciprocal carrier. The centralizer of a complex structure on a real two-plane is the complex algebra; its invertible transports are nevertheless not closed under the addition needed for destructive interference at a selected output. A one-outcome dephaser and a reversible phase kick show that certainty of a public outcome does not imply nondisturbance. Within an explicitly assumed positive quadratic response class, calibrated zero-leakage comparisons on a connected tree select the all-ones Gram kernel. We prove quantitative stability estimates and retain the distinction between unconditional record-local operations, selective conditioning, and a controlled inverse interaction. A local normalized-weight ansatz, arbitrary anchored orthogonal refinement, and additive coarse-graining of recorded outcomes force weights proportional to quadratic size. Equal binary refinement alone does not: a positive continuous increasing log-periodic function supplies a counterexample, while binary and ternary refinement at every size, with continuity, recover linearity in size. Real rotations implement the requisite refinements and controlled tagging without full complex control. Fixed-complement preservation and reciprocal reversal admit different generator sectors; independently switchable real mixers on a connected graph plus one addressable local phase generate the unitary Lie algebra. A common scalar phase does not. Supplementary finite action–phase and history examples expose the remaining calibration assumptions. The contribution is an assumption-controlled synthesis of conditional selectors and obstructions, not the introduction of the standard component constructions. The carrier, comparison response, control resources, tensor interpretation, and empirical probability interface are not derived from primitive complementarity.
