## Abstract

As part of Complementarity-First research and its Reciprocal Internal Complementarity (RIC) program, we study the smooth-core requirements of an admitted magnetic sector. A compact phase bundle can carry integral flux without selecting a nonzero sector or a dynamical particle. When its opposite-weight carrier is equipped with an identity-covering antiunitary involution of square +I, the fixed real plane is the underlying real bundle of the charged line. Its linking-sphere Euler number is the product of representation weight and magnetic degree. Complex first-Chern-class cancellation therefore does not remove the same-rank reciprocal extension obstruction. On the sphere, neutral stabilization removes only even winding; an additional odd charged real plane is the minimal direct-sum enlargement for an odd sector. A compatible complex rank-four realization admits a supplied SU(2) connection, while the original charged splitting fails at the scalar zero. The geometric result is separated from the choice of a classical action and from the observables to be recovered. A standard massless-scalar core has exact projected flux but fails relative Maxwell-only spatial-stress matching. For a separately supplied canonical adjoint-scalar model with equal scalar and vector masses, a rational core-to-tail contraction and an on-shell energy estimate give a certificate-qualified radial completion. Its reduced energy obeys 1.2376844 < Ered < 1.2377150. Complete relative exterior energy-density and spatial-stress errors are below 0.000789 for ρ ≥ 8; the transport discrepancy is at most π/700 under the stated finite path budget. The proofs and certificate conditions are given here, with machine-readable inputs and recorded outputs accompanying the manuscript. These conclusions do not select the action, coupling, physical mass scale, particle population, or dynamical stability. A local exchange-control application preserves the distinction between representation compatibility and field dynamics.
