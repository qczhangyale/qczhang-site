## Abstract

A finite amplitude dynamics, a configuration-space propagator, and a mechanical action are different mathematical objects. We give a conditional one-dimensional bridge between them while retaining the assumptions that make each identification possible. Common-additive-shift invariance selects a relative two-mode quadratic energy; a supplied first-order kinetic term and action scale turn its graph Laplacian into reciprocal norm-preserving dynamics. The resulting amplitude action vanishes on solutions and therefore cannot simply be identified with the configuration action. A convergent ordered-jump expansion retains diagonal dwell phases, hopping factors, and time-simplex measure. On a supplied spatial lattice, inverse-square scaling gives second-order fixed-band convergence and strong convergence for each fixed square-integrable state, but not all-mode real-time operator-norm convergence. The raw return-kernel density has two distinct mesh-subsequence limits. A declared Gaussian resolution instead gives an explicit uniform lattice-to-continuum bound, separate finite-volume control, and locally uniform removal of resolution at nonzero time. With independently declared mass and action calibration, the limit has the free mechanical action and exact finite-slice normalization. For smooth positive variable couplings, midpoint edges and uniform site measure select a divergence-form operator. Different measures or orderings retain the same classical principal symbol while changing lower-order terms. The Liouville coordinate and amplitude transformation fixes a local correction, endpoint factors, and the internal measure; these must be carried together. Smooth-orbit lattice and state-level time-slicing estimates give controlled comparisons without asserting a raw variable-coefficient kernel limit. The contribution is the organized conditional bridge and its obstructions, not the invention of the standard component methods or a derivation of geometry, the action-scale value, physical couplings, or empirical probability from primitive complementarity.
