## Abstract

A partial-transpose image of the positive-semidefinite cone supplies an established alternative ordered presentation on a fixed composite carrier. Transporting both the cone and the processes preserves an abstract equivalence, whereas fixing the process representation can discriminate the two orders. We study the iteration of one specified right-argument transpose rule on supplied finite qubit carriers. With three standard leaves, its two bracketings have different cones, so no linear identification fixing every product operator pointwise can make them agree. Subset-orientation labels give an explicit repair. Relative to a separately supplied tensor-closed standard process category, we construct the raw tensor of arrows, associator, symmetry, asymmetric unit maps, and quasi-inverse tensorator, and prove symmetric monoidal equivalence by faithful transport. Complementary subsets define the same isolated cone, but their identification is not a congruence for the raw tensor product. At fixed rank, a precisely defined two-lift groupoid is a split central Z₂ extension; its odd lift is not an ordinary completely positive endomorphism at fixed representatives. A five-qubit example then separates matched reference transport, a fixed calibrated anchor, and an effect-based quotient. The latter loses a linear subspace rather than only a two-point transpose orbit and is preserved only by histories satisfying the standard kernel-invariance condition. A named real local unitary connects the anchor pair, while a real controlled-NOT (CNOT) gate exposes the limitation of an enlarged single-anchor history class. The contribution is an assumption-controlled analysis of one finite composition rule and its interfaces, not a new general coherence, orientation, or resource theorem, and not a derivation of quantum theory from primitive complementarity.
