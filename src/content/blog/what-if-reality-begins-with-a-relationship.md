Imagine two quantum systems that appear identical under every publicly permitted test.

Every detector clicks with the same probability. Every local measurement agrees. Different observers follow a fully declared protocol, compare their records and reach the same conclusion: within everything they are allowed to measure, there is no physical difference between the systems.

Then the experimental interface is enlarged to admit a global measurement.

The supposed twins separate.

In one exact finite model, two distinct quantum states give precisely the same probability—$1/9$—for every outcome in the complete declared product-measurement interface. Yet a global measurement distinguishes them with total-variation distance $3/5$, corresponding to an equal-prior success probability of $4/5$.

The missing information was not unreal. It was not mathematically undefined. It was inaccessible only relative to the original architecture of observation.

That result offers an unusually concrete entrance into **[Complementarity-First Foundational Release I](/)**, a coordinated research corpus developed by independent researcher Qingchun Zhang. The release consists of nine technical papers and one overview, spanning 245 technical-manuscript pages. It moves from a proposed relational primitive through quantum foundations, finite and local gravity constructions, and a bounded attempt at Unified Dynamics.

Its central question is simple enough to state and difficult enough to reorganize foundational physics:

> **What if reality does not begin with separate things that later enter into relationships? What if it begins with a completed relationship whose distinguishable roles exist only through that completion?**

Particles, states, observers and spacetime would then not necessarily be the first ingredients in nature’s inventory. They might be later structures—stable roles and patterns that emerge from something relationally prior.

The proposal is ambitious. But its most important rule is restraint:

> **Nothing is allowed to emerge by verbal suggestion alone.**

Probability must be derived or explicitly supplied. So must complex numbers, dimensionality, geometry, transport, dynamics, causal orientation and physical scale.

The project calls such additional choices **selectors**, and it treats the exposure of those selectors as part of the scientific result.

## The spark: from Tao to DNA

Complementarity-First did not begin with an anomalous particle track or an unexplained astronomical signal.

It began with an ancient generative sequence.

Chapter 42 of the *Tao Te Ching* contains the line

> 道生一，一生二，二生三，三生萬物。

The working translation used in the foundational paper is:

> “The Tao gives rise to One; One gives rise to Two; Two gives rise to Three; Three gives rise to the myriad things.”

The project does not assign the Tao a physical variable, and it does not suggest that Laozi secretly anticipated quantum mechanics or general relativity. The line instead provoked a structural question:

**Must multiplicity begin as a catalogue of already separate, self-sufficient entities? Or might the many arise through successive differentiation within a prior unity?**

That is an order of inspiration, not a deductive argument. The formal theory does not yet contain a theorem deriving Two from One or the many from Three. Its mathematics begins only after differentiation has already occurred.

A second inspiration came from the **Taichi Diagram**, the Diagram of the Supreme Ultimate. Its significance for the program lies in the possibility of a unity that contains distinguishable and mutually constraining aspects without decomposing into two independently complete substances.

The foundational paper compresses this idea into a phrase:

> **difference without separability**

The roles are genuinely different. Yet what they are, and what they can do, belongs to a larger completion.

A third inspiration came from a radically different source: **double-stranded DNA**.

The two strands of DNA are chemically distinguishable and antiparallel. The sequence of each constrains the sequence of its complement. During semiconservative replication, either parental strand can serve as a template. Differentiation, reciprocal constraint and generative potential coexist within one paired architecture.

But DNA also supplied the decisive warning.

Complementarity alone does not replicate a genome. Copying requires enzymes, substrates, energy, strand discrimination, proofreading and repair. The architecture creates a structured possibility; additional machinery determines what actually occurs.

That biological lesson became a methodological rule for the physics program. A complementary relation may provide a structural seed, but it cannot silently be credited with producing quantum probability, spacetime or dynamics. Every added mechanism must be named. Every selector must be exposed. Every claimed emergence must be demonstrated.

The *Tao Te Ching*, the Taichi Diagram and DNA therefore do not prove Complementarity-First. They explain why the question was asked—and why the resulting program is so strict about the difference between inspiration and derivation.

## The radical reversal

Most physical theories begin with nouns.

Particles move through space. Fields occupy spacetime. Quantum states belong to systems. Observers perform measurements. Even highly abstract theories usually begin with entities already typed well enough to possess states, locations, symmetries or dynamics.

Complementarity-First reverses that order.

Its proposed primitive is a **completed relation**, written schematically as

$$
\mathcal C=[a\mathrel{\dashv}b].
$$

Here $a$ and $b$ do not name two independently complete objects that existed first and were connected afterward. They identify distinguishable role-positions internal to the completed relation $\mathcal C$.

The relation is therefore not merely a bridge between two preexisting things. Within the proposed explanatory order, the distinguishable “sides” are disclosed by the completion itself.

This is the **Principle of Complementary Relational Primacy**:

> A completed complementary relation is treated as logically prior to assigning independent physical completeness to its distinguishable internal roles.

The word *prior* is not temporal. The proposal is not that a relationship existed for a few seconds and then produced objects. The priority is explanatory and ontological within the model: begin with the completed relation, then investigate how object-like carriers, compositions, geometries and dynamics might arise from it.

This is where the program’s restraint becomes crucial.

A completed relation does not automatically tell us whether its mathematical carrier should be real, complex or quaternionic. It does not determine dimensionality, continuity, probability, causal signature, an action principle or an absolute physical scale. Elementary independence models in the foundational paper show that many such structures can vary while the primitive relational proposal remains unchanged.

The bold move is therefore not simply to say, “relationships come first.”

The stronger move is to ask, at every stage:

> **What follows from the relation—and what has just been added?**

## A quantum fact that waits for reunion

One of the clearest answers appears in the release’s study of **rebits**, the real-number counterparts of qubits.

A two-rebit state is represented within a 10-dimensional real symmetric state space. But the complete span of real product measurements occupies only nine dimensions.

One direction is missing.

The omitted coordinate can be written

$$
s(\rho)=\operatorname{tr}\!\left[(J\otimes J)\rho\right].
$$

It is globally meaningful, but separated real-local product measurements have no direct linear sensitivity to it for arbitrary mixed states under the paper’s copy-depth-one resource model.

The obvious question is no longer philosophical. It is experimental:

> **What new resource makes the hidden coordinate visible?**

The answer depends on how the experiment is organized.

If the two laboratories remain separated, use only real-local processing and terminate in product measurements, the coordinate stays hidden at coherent copy depth one. But after the systems reunite, the paper gives two exact readout procedures.

One applies a connected real joint pulse and then reads a single local population. The other uses a single CNOT gate followed by ordinary local measurements and a parity calculation.

For the CNOT route,

$$
C(J\otimes J)C^{T}=X\otimes Z,
$$

so the hidden quantity becomes accessible through a product-basis parity statistic:

$$
s(\rho)=2p_{XZ}^{(+)}-1.
$$

The joint gate does not create the missing coordinate. It **transduces** an already existing global quantity into an observable population or parity signal.

The resource hierarchy becomes even richer when copies and reference frames are considered. Two identical copies allow separated observers to recover $|s|$, the magnitude of the coordinate. Yet its sign remains tied to an oriented reference under the stated real-local restrictions.

The result suggests a broader principle:

> **Observability is not merely a property of a state. It is a property of a state together with an interface, a control topology, a copy depth and a reference structure.**

A fact can be invisible under one architecture and exactly measurable under another.

The hidden two-rebit coordinate does not, by itself, explain why quantum probabilities exist. But it gives concrete form to a broader conjecture that motivated Complementarity-First from its early quantum work: perhaps the state available to an observer is only a restricted image of a more complete complementary relational state.

In that picture, the complete whole contains both the accessible system and a complementary partner, reference, environment, record or relational sector that the observer cannot directly interrogate. Restricting access to that complement produces the state available locally; a measurement of that accessible state then produces an observed probability. Probability could therefore be understood, informally, as the readout of a **“projection of a projection”**—first from the complete relational whole to an accessible state, and then from that state to an outcome probability. The second step is not necessarily another geometric projection; it is the operational evaluation performed by a measurement. Release I presents this as a motivating conjecture, not a completed derivation: it has not yet shown that primitive complementarity forces a pure coherent completion, the Born rule, or complex rather than real quantum theory.

## What a selector actually does

The word *selector* can sound almost magical—as though a selector were a hidden force that creates whatever physical structure appears after it is chosen.

That is not how the term is used in Complementarity-First.

A selector may choose a mathematical carrier, admit a class of operations, fix a real form, select a branch of a logarithm, impose a boundary condition, identify a measurement interface or supply an operational principle. It narrows a space of possibilities or opens a channel through which an existing structure can have physical consequences.

A useful schematic is

$$
\boxed{
\text{observed result}
=
\text{carrier and state}
+
\text{relational structure}
+
\text{selector}
+
\text{dynamics}
+
\text{readout}.
}
$$

No one term in that expression is generally the sole cause.

The two-rebit example makes this distinction exact.

Suppose a state has $s(\rho)\neq0$, but the systems remain separated and no suitable joint operation is permitted. The coordinate exists, yet it remains hidden from product measurements.

Suppose instead that a CNOT is available, but the state happens to have $s(\rho)=0$. The gate does not create a hidden-coordinate signal out of nothing.

Only when the state carries a nonzero coordinate, the experimental rules permit a suitable joint interaction, the interaction rotates that coordinate into a visible sector and an appropriate measurement is performed does the signal appear.

The selector therefore does not manufacture the quantity.

> **The selector opens the channel; it does not manufacture what passes through the channel.**

This point matters well beyond the readout experiment.

The core quantum-reconstruction paper studies a conditional route to finite-dimensional complex quantum theory. At its most compressed, the theorem-use structure contains three parts:

- an ambient causal operational framework, denoted $AOPT$;
- a local-equivalence principle, $L$;
- an equivalent-system purification principle, $E$.

Finite classical probability theory provides a model with $AOPT+L$ but without $E$. Finite-dimensional real quantum theory provides a model with $AOPT+E$ but without $L$. Neither selector premise alone is sufficient in the comparison family. The full package $AOPT+L+E$ closes only through an external reconstruction theorem.

And none of $AOPT$, $L$ or $E$ is derived from primitive complementarity in Release I.

That makes the logical status precise.

The selectors are indispensable within the conditional theorem, but they are not the sole cause of complex quantum theory. The conclusion depends on the ambient operational framework, the meanings of states and transformations, the composite structure, the two selector principles and the external theorem connecting the complete package to the target formalism.

The same lesson appears in the Born rule.

Binary complementarity and normalization are not enough to force the standard quantum probability law. Additional structure—such as mixture affinity or, in another bounded route, independent-product factorization together with continuity—is needed to obtain a unique local trace rule at the stated scope.

So the selector discipline cuts both ways.

It prevents the project from pretending that primitive complementarity has already derived quantum mechanics. But it also prevents the opposite mistake of imagining that the selector alone created the result.

Release I often establishes statements of the form:

> Given this carrier, this state space, these structural relations and this declared selector package, the following consequence holds.

The harder future question is:

> Why should nature select that package?

That question remains open wherever the selector has not itself been derived.

## When agreement is not completeness

The companion objectivity paper asks a question at the heart of science:

What does it mean for a result to be objective?

One plausible answer is operational. A result is objective when qualified observers can follow a public procedure, calibrate their apparatus, preserve the provenance of their evidence, reproduce the experiment and agree on the outcome.

But does successful public agreement guarantee that the underlying physical state has been completely identified?

The two-rebit countermodel says no.

The paper constructs a detailed public certificate involving declared access, calibrated interventions, relative alignment, evidence dependence, reproducibility and controlled revision. The same fixed public record passes the certificate for two distinct states. Every implemented product outcome has probability $1/9$ for both.

The public agreement is genuine. The protocol is not meaningless. The observers are not mistaken about the evidence they actually possess.

Yet the public evaluation map is **noninjective**: more than one underlying state generates the same complete public record. A separately admitted global query then distinguishes the states with total-variation distance $3/5$ and equal-prior success $4/5$.

This separates two achievements that are often treated as one.

**Operational objectivity** means that observers can publicly establish and reproduce a result relative to a declared interface.

**Tomographic exhaustiveness** means that the interface uniquely determines the complete state.

The first does not guarantee the second.

A scientific map can be reliable without being one-to-one with the territory. Two distinct physical possibilities may cast exactly the same shadow through one observation channel while separating sharply through another.

The paper does not infer a unique hidden ontology from what the public interface misses. Its conclusion is more disciplined: objectivity can be real, reproducible and scientifically useful while remaining relative to a nonexhaustive interface.

## A clock can tick in a universe with no arrow

Another paper turns from observability to time.

Relational approaches can describe change without assuming an external universal clock. One subsystem acts as a clock, and the state of another is described relative to its reading.

That may explain how events can be ordered.

It does not yet explain why time appears to have a direction.

A film running backward still contains an ordered succession of frames. What makes it look wrong is not the absence of sequence. It is the behavior of records, heat and waste: smoke returns to a candle, fragments assemble into a glass, memories disappear toward what we normally call the future.

A readable clock is therefore not the same thing as a thermodynamic arrow.

The relational-time paper separates resources that are often compressed into the single phrase “the emergence of time”:

- a clock that can distinguish configurations;
- a writable memory;
- unused storage capacity;
- a mechanism that protects records;
- error detection and repair;
- a controller that orders repair operations;
- organized fuel;
- somewhere for error information and waste to go;
- and, for sustained operation, an effective open boundary.

A reversible interaction can create records. It can also erase them by running backward. A finite controller can maintain a record for a long interval and still eventually recur. A subsystem can become more entropic because correlations have moved elsewhere, even when the complete dynamics remains reversible.

The paper’s most important conceptual shift is this:

> **The arrow of time is not supplied merely by a variable that tells time. It depends on the physical economy of creating, protecting and exporting records.**

The past is not only what comes earlier in an ordering. It is what has left stable evidence.

In a closed finite architecture, record maintenance cannot consume blank capacity and export fresh error information forever without confronting finite storage, recurrence or waste accumulation. But the paper does not claim that every finite memory has a universal expiration date, nor that an open driven system cannot continue operating while fresh resources and waste channels remain available.

## Can spacetime be built from incidence?

The gravity branch applies the same discipline to an even larger question.

Instead of assuming spacetime and placing physical relations inside it, the program asks whether geometry can be reconstructed from **incidence**: structured information about which mathematical elements meet, overlap or stand in a null relation.

But complementarity alone does not generate four-dimensional Lorentzian spacetime.

The construction first selects a rank-two paired-chiral carrier, a suitable real structure, orientation and time orientation, an affine vector or displacement sector and laws of transport. These are not hidden in the notation; they are recorded as selectors.

Once that scaffolding is supplied, a striking chain becomes possible.

On an appropriate Hermitian real slice, line incidence becomes a determinant-null condition. The polarization of that determinant supplies a Lorentzian conformal metric. Additional simplicity, gluing and nondegeneracy conditions then permit common-coframe data to emerge conditionally.

The program also distinguishes two effects that are often blurred together:

- **curvature**, detected by how a frame changes around a loop;
- **torsion or coframe nonclosure**, detected by whether transported displacement vectors close.

After further assumptions, the construction reaches a discrete first-order Palatini–Regge architecture related to general relativity.

The most recognizable endpoint appears on one frozen periodic lattice. Away from its characteristic cone, the physical metric quotient has dimension six. On the nonzero characteristic cone, the relevant rank falls by two, leaving exactly **two physical null polarizations**—the familiar polarization count of classical gravitational radiation.

In the same finite construction, eliminating the independent connection produces a response that agrees coefficient by coefficient with the corresponding Regge Hessian on every frozen momentum-space fiber.

These results are not yet quantum gravitons. They are not an arbitrary-mesh theorem, a continuum limit or a derivation of nonlinear Einstein dynamics.

But they are a nontrivial demonstration that a carefully selected relational-incidence architecture can reach a gravitational structure with an unexpectedly familiar signature.

And once again, the selectors are not the sole cause. Rank-two chiral modules do not by themselves generate gravity. A Hermitian real structure does not by itself produce dynamics. A local logarithm branch does not by itself create curvature.

The result arises only from the complete architecture: incidence, carrier, reality condition, orientation, displacement data, transport, simplicity, gluing, action assumptions, branch control and finite realization.

## The shortcut that failed—and made the theory stronger

A project with such large ambitions might be tempted to hide its unsuccessful routes.

Complementarity-First instead turns them into part of the architecture.

A scalar “helix phase” was investigated as a possible universal transport variable. In the local family tested, it did not select a unique physical global phase. More general matrix-valued, nonlocal or cycle-level mechanisms remain possible, but the simple shortcut failed.

A broad gravity-response transfer failed even more dramatically. Instead of erasing that result, the program preserved it and asked what local source information the failed rule had overlooked.

The changed geometry carried incidence and normalization information that the broad transfer rule had not encoded. More refined local descriptors were therefore developed and tested under explicit evidence chronology. Later prospective tests froze the relevant geometry, descriptor and predictions before the target responses were examined.

The resulting successes remain finite, exact-class and admission-bound. They do not become a universal amplitude law for unseen geometries or an arbitrary-triangulation theorem.

This is one of the release’s most valuable ideas.

A theory should not be judged only by the number of positive statements it can make. It should also be judged by how precisely it records the conditions under which those statements stop being true.

A separate gravity paper studies the opposite problem.

The source-sufficiency work asks why a **nonzero** local response can be transferred when the right local information is supplied.

The six-sector paper asks why a local response must be **exactly zero** under a specific symmetry.

For a complete six-sector orbit with exact $S_3$ equivariance, Reynolds averaging isolates the invariant component. If that component is absent, the sector contributions cancel exactly. The result is a finite representation-theoretic theorem, not a universal explanation for every zero response.

One branch studies what information is sufficient for a nonzero answer. The other studies what symmetry forces the answer to vanish.

Keeping them separate prevents a seductive but false summary such as “the gravity calculations worked.”

Some worked. Some failed. Some vanish for exact reasons. Some transfer only under stated descriptors. Their different statuses are part of the physics.

## A common grammar for quantum theory and gravity

The Unified Dynamics paper asks whether the quantum and gravity branches can be placed inside one finite relational architecture.

It identifies a real dual-pair carrier and a common **BF-type kernel**, a first-order pairing between field-like and curvature-like variables.

This common kernel supports two different descendants.

In the gravity branch, the relevant field is constrained into a simple or tetradic form built from coframe data. Varying the coframe permits a curved gravitational sector.

In the compact relational branch, the analogous variable is treated as an independent multiplier-like degree of freedom. Its variation imposes a different condition.

The same action direction can therefore host both branches—but only because the branches use different admissible configuration and variation spaces.

The common kernel does not explain why nature should choose one variation space in the gravity regime and the other in the quantum-relational regime.

The paper calls this the **variation-space selector obstruction**.

The result is best understood as a shared grammar rather than a completed language.

Two theories may share deep syntax without sharing every permitted expression. Finding the common grammar is significant. It does not yet determine which physical sentences nature is allowed to speak.

The paper therefore assigns its maturity conservatively:

- structural unification is closed only at finite and local scope;
- reduction unification remains partial;
- dynamical unification is partial but advanced;
- predictive unification remains on hold.

The architecture does not yet derive an absolute length or duration, Newton’s constant, the cosmological constant, particle masses or a parameter-free experimental prediction.

This is another place where selector language must be handled carefully.

The unresolved variation-space selector is not imagined as a button that would single-handedly “cause” quantum theory or gravity. Even if such a principle were discovered, it would act inside an already elaborate architecture. Its role would be to explain why one admissible dynamical realization is physically selected over another.

The common carrier and common kernel provide structured possibilities.

The missing selector would explain the branch.

## Why the missing pieces are exciting

A scientific program becomes powerful not only when it supplies answers, but when it transforms vague mysteries into sharp problems.

Release I ends with a ledger of precisely such problems.

How does one completed relation compose into a many-body world?

Can probability—and specifically the Born rule—be derived from relational structure rather than supplied through an operational selector?

Can local incompleteness and global completion force nonseparable states? Can purification emerge rather than be postulated? Why should physical amplitudes be complex rather than real, quaternionic or part of a more general Jordan structure?

Can the quantum operation of orthocomplementation, the exchange of dual relational roles and gravitational parity be proved to descend from one primitive complementary operation?

What selects rank two, four-dimensional Lorentzian reality, orientation and time orientation?

Is there a global transport law more powerful than the local scalar helix mechanisms already tested?

Can the finite gravity constructions survive arbitrary meshes, global gluing, refinement and a continuum limit?

What chooses the different quantum and gravitational variation spaces inside the common BF-type kernel?

And what additional physical input could break the remaining positive-scaling freedom, determine absolute constants and produce a parameter-free empirical prediction?

These are not afterthoughts. They are the frontier created by the release.

The formal overview classifies nine release-level problems as open, two as open with promotion on hold and external scholarly validation and replication as not yet performed.

Before the program, one might have asked vaguely whether “everything comes from complementarity.” After Release I, that question has fractured into a sequence of mathematical and operational tasks, each with a declared burden of proof.

That is progress even where closure has not yet been reached.

## The unusual scientific claim: failures belong in the architecture

Perhaps the most distinctive feature of Release I is not any one equation. It is the insistence that the maturity of a result must remain visible.

A conceptual proposal is labeled as a conceptual proposal.

A theorem that depends on six hypotheses is not summarized without those hypotheses.

A construction discovered after a response was seen is not retroactively described as prospective.

A failed transfer is preserved rather than rewritten as a success.

An exact finite calculation is not promoted to a continuum law.

A public certificate is not confused with full tomography.

An internally separated audit is not called external replication.

A selector that completes a conditional theorem is not described as though primitive complementarity had already derived it.

This “anti-flattening” rule addresses a recurring problem in foundational physics. Large programs often combine philosophy, mathematics, numerical experiments and physical interpretation. Once compressed into one dramatic narrative, conditional results can begin to sound unconditional, suggestive analogies can be remembered as derivations and a successful special case can quietly become a universal law.

Complementarity-First instead treats the boundary of a result as part of the result.

The overview does not take ownership of the technical theorems. Each claim remains assigned to the paper that establishes it, with its evidence type and nonclaims preserved.

## A different kind of foundational ambition

Complementarity-First begins with a radical possibility:

Perhaps relations are not secondary connections among preexisting things. Perhaps the things of physics are stable role-structures that emerge within deeper completed relations.

Release I does not prove that this is how nature ultimately works.

What it does is more concrete.

It turns the possibility into a controlled reconstruction program.

It identifies quantum information that is invisible to one interface and measurable through another.

It separates public objectivity from complete state determination.

It shows why a relational clock does not manufacture a thermodynamic arrow.

It constructs a conditional path from incidence to finite Lorentzian and Regge geometry.

It finds a shared quantum–gravity action direction and names the selector obstruction that still prevents full dynamical unification.

And it clarifies what selectors actually do.

They do not create an entire theory from nothing. They choose among structured possibilities, open operational channels, impose admissibility conditions or identify physical branches. Their effects remain inseparable from the carriers, states, dynamics and measurements on which they act.

Most importantly, the program makes hidden assumptions visible.

Physics advances when a new equation succeeds. But it also advances when an assumption that had been doing silent work is dragged into the light and turned into a question that can be derived, tested, rejected or shown to be indispensable.

That is the wager of Complementarity-First:

> **Begin with relation. Add nothing silently. Follow each selector to its consequences. Preserve the failures. And ask how much of the physical world can genuinely be rebuilt.**
